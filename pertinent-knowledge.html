K53 Essential Knowledge Guide

Quick-reference figures and principles from the academy's supplied K53 Essential Knowledge Guide.

Important source note: The figures and statements below are transcribed and lightly formatted from the supplied academy document. Some statements may be outdated, simplified, or context-dependent. This page preserves the source as a study reference; verify current legal requirements with official South African guidance and follow road signs and traffic officers.

1. Learner licence ages and validity

  • Motorcycle 125 cc or less without sidecar: minimum age 16 years.
  • Light motor vehicle not more than 3,500 kg and motorcycle exceeding 125 cc: minimum age 17 years (as stated in the supplied document).
  • Heavy and extra-heavy: minimum age 18 years.
  • Learner’s licence validity: 24 months.
  • Clearance certificate/licence disc validity: 12 months.

2. Following distance and observation

  • Light motor vehicles: 2 seconds.
  • Motorcycles: 2 seconds.
  • Heavy motor vehicles: 3 seconds.
  • Increase following distances in bad light and bad weather.
  • Safety ideal frequency of checking mirrors while driving: every 5–8 seconds.

3. Stopping and parking distances

  • Pedestrian crossing: no stopping or parking inside, on, or closer than 9 metres.
  • Next to or opposite a vehicle where road width is less than 9 metres: no stopping/parking as stated in the source.
  • Where the road has been narrowed to 6 metres: no stopping/parking as stated in the source.
  • Tunnel, subway, bridge or railway reserve: 6 metres.
  • Parking restrictions in urban areas where road width is less than 5.5 metres; intersection: 5 metres; fire hydrant: 1.5 metres; road edge outside urban area: 1 metre; kerbside wheel outer edge not more than 450 mm into the road.

4. Lights and hooters

  • Headlights must be switched on when visibility is less than 150 metres.
  • Main/bright beam must light objects ahead at least 100 metres.
  • Dipped beam should strike the road surface not further ahead than 45 metres.
  • Parking lights must be on if a vehicle is on the roadside outside a parking area or more than 12 metres from an illuminated street sign (as stated in the source).
  • Hooter must be audible at least 90 metres.

5. Towing

  • Maximum distance between two vehicles being towed: 3.5 metres.
  • A red flag is required to show the rope when towing distance is more than 1.8 metres (as stated in the source).
  • Maximum speed when towing with a rope: 30 km/h.

6. Collisions

  • Report accidents to police within 24 hours.
  • Display the safety triangle at least 45 metres behind the vehicle.
  • If anyone is killed or injured, mark the vehicles’ positions before moving them out of the roadway, as described in the source.

7. Tyres

  • Vehicle tyre tread depth: at least 1 mm (as stated in the source).
  • Motorcycle tyre tread pattern: at least 80% (as stated in the source).
  • No motorcycle may have retreaded tyres (as stated in the source).

8. Alcohol limits listed in the source

  • Ordinary driver: blood alcohol maximum 0.05 g per 100 ml of blood; breath maximum 0.24 mg per 1,000 ml of breath.
  • Professional driver: blood alcohol maximum 0.02 g per 100 ml of blood; breath maximum 0.10 mg per 1,000 ml of breath.

9. General speed limits listed in the source

  • Urban roads: 60 km/h.
  • Rural and national roads: 100 km/h.
  • Freeways: 120 km/h.
  • Vehicle combinations over 9,000 kg: 80 km/h.
  • Buses and minibuses conveying passengers: 100 km/h.

10. Loads and projections

  • Goods may not extend in front of the vehicle by more than 300 mm; source also lists 600 mm to the front of the front axle.
  • Goods may not extend behind the vehicle by more than 1.8 m; source also lists 900 mm beyond the rear axle.
  • Goods on a car roof may not be higher than half the car’s height.
  • The source lists goods width limits of 2.5 m and, for vehicles above 12,000 kg, 2.6 m.
  • Motorcycle loads: source lists 450 mm beyond the side of the cycle wheels and 300 mm beyond the sidecar wheels.

11. Defensive driving

  • Defensive driving aims to keep the space around the vehicle safe and reduce collision risk.
  • Hazards are stationary (such as poles, trees, parked vehicles and intersections), moving (such as pedestrians, animals and vehicles), or changing (such as weather and light).
  • Technique: Search for hazards; Identify how they may affect speed or direction; Predict their potential and the effect of your actions; Decide on the safest course of action.

Source: Coki Driving School, Amended K53 Essential Knowledge Guide, pages 6–8.
